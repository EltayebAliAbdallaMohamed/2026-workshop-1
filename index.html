The Role of Verbs in Vocabulary Learning

Why Q Skills Word Lists Should Include More Verbs

Eltayeb Mohamed — Q Skills Intro, Section 66740

Background

Q Skills for Success (Oxford University Press) provides key vocabulary lists at the back of each textbook. However, these lists often contain very few verbs—and sometimes none at all. This creates a gap in how learners infer meaning, especially when encountering new nouns.

Why This Matters

For language learners—especially visually impaired students—verbs are powerful cognitive anchors. They help students:

  • Identify the role of an unfamiliar noun
  • Predict the general category of meaning
  • Understand how the noun behaves in real contexts
  • Build mental models of events and actions

Verbs Provide Context

When learners hear a sentence like:

  • "She wears a ___."
  • "He drives a ___."
  • "They cook the ___."

The verb immediately narrows the possibilities and helps listening comprehension.

Verbs Reveal Word Function

Verbs tell learners how the unknown noun participates in an action:

  • Is it something you hold?
  • Something you visit?
  • Something you eat?
  • Something you fix?

Cognitive Benefits

  • Activate semantic networks
  • Improve memory retention
  • Strengthen contextual guessing skills
  • Support top-down processing during listening

For visually impaired learners, verbs provide auditory scaffolding that replaces missing visual cues.

Problem in Q Skills

  • Focus heavily on nouns and adjectives
  • Provide limited action vocabulary
  • Reduce opportunities for context-based inference
  • Make listening tasks harder for beginners

Example: Weak Context

Word list:

  • apartment
  • neighbor
  • noise

Without verbs, students struggle to understand what happens or how these nouns relate.

Example: Strong Context

Add verbs:

  • live
  • complain
  • hear

Now learners can infer relationships: people live in apartments; neighbors may complain; you hear noise.

Recommendation

  • Add high-frequency verbs to each unit’s word list
  • Pair nouns with typical collocating verbs
  • Include verbs in listening pre-teaching
  • Provide verb-noun frames (e.g., wear + clothing)

Classroom Application

  • Introduce verbs before nouns
  • Use guiding questions (e.g., "What do you usually wear?")
  • Create verb-based cloze listening tasks
  • Encourage guessing nouns from actions

Impact on Learners

  • Improved listening comprehension
  • Better vocabulary retention
  • Stronger contextual guessing
  • Increased confidence and accessibility

Conclusion

Verbs are not optional in vocabulary learning. They are essential scaffolds that help learners infer meaning, categorize new words, and understand real-world usage. Q Skills word lists should integrate verbs to support all learners.
